# Use-Case Prompts

Organized by what you are making rather than by genre. Each entry includes the practical constraints that matter for that context — length, whether vocals help or hurt, and whether it needs to loop.

The constraint line is often more important than the prompt. A perfect track that fades out when you needed a seamless loop is unusable.

## Podcast intro

**Constraints:** 10–30 seconds · no vocals (they compete with speech) · needs a clean, definite ending

```
Modern indie, confident, no vocals, 110 BPM, plucked synth motif, light percussion, short memorable hook, clean ending
```

`118 chars`

Ask for a **motif**, not a song. An intro needs one recognisable three-second idea, repeated — not a verse-chorus structure compressed into fifteen seconds.

**Outro variant** — same palette, reversed energy:

```
Modern indie, warm, no vocals, 110 BPM, same plucked motif slowed, strips back to single instrument, clean fade
```

`111 chars`

## YouTube / vlog background

**Constraints:** 2–5 minutes · no vocals, or wordless only · must survive being ducked under speech

```
Chill house, upbeat, no vocals, 118 BPM, warm bass, light plucks, no big drops, even energy throughout, mid-range light
```

`119 chars`

`mid-range light` is the key fragment. Background music competes with the human voice in the 200 Hz–4 kHz band; a track with an uncrowded midrange stays audible without masking speech.

`no big drops` prevents the track from pulling attention at the wrong moment.

## Short-form video

**Constraints:** 15–60 seconds · hook must land in the first 3 seconds · vocals optional but a hook helps

```
Hyperpop, playful, chopped female vocal, 148 BPM, immediate hook, bright synths, no intro, starts at full energy
```

`112 chars`

`no intro, starts at full energy` is the whole trick. Short-form video has no room for a build — the first second must already be the payoff.

**Trending-sound variant:**

```
Phonk, aggressive, no vocals, 140 BPM, distorted 808, cowbell melody, memphis vocal chop, instant hook, loop-friendly
```

`117 chars`

## Game background loop

**Constraints:** seamless loop · no vocals · must tolerate hundreds of repetitions

```
Ambient orchestral, curious, no vocals, 84 BPM, light strings, celeste, soft woodwind, loop-friendly, no ending, no drums
```

`121 chars`

`loop-friendly, no ending` is mandatory. So is restraint: any element distinctive enough to be noticed on the first loop becomes intolerable by the fortieth. Avoid strong melodic hooks in exploration music.

**Combat variant:**

```
Orchestral action, urgent, no vocals, 140 BPM, driving percussion, brass stabs, ostinato strings, loops seamlessly
```

`114 chars`

**Menu / title variant:**

```
Ambient, anticipatory, no vocals, 70 BPM, sustained pads, single piano motif, spacious, loops seamlessly, very calm
```

`115 chars`

## Film trailer

**Constraints:** 60–150 seconds · three-act dynamic arc · no vocals except wordless choir

```
Epic trailer, ominous to triumphant, wordless choir, 90 BPM, braams, riser hits, war drums, three builds, final impact
```

`118 chars`

`three builds` matches the standard trailer structure: setup, escalation, final hit. `ominous to triumphant` describes an arc rather than a state, which is the only way to get one.

`braams` is the industry term for the low brass hit — models trained on music text recognise it.

## Advertising / commercial

**Constraints:** 15/30/60-second cuts · must not distract from voiceover · brand-safe and generic-positive

```
Upbeat corporate pop, optimistic, no vocals, 122 BPM, bright plucks, handclaps, simple build, clean 30-second arc
```

`113 chars`

Name the length in the prompt when the cut is fixed. `clean 30-second arc` produces a more usable result than a 3-minute track you have to edit down.

**Premium / luxury variant:**

```
Minimal cinematic, refined, no vocals, 90 BPM, sparse piano, subtle strings, lots of space, understated, no percussion
```

`118 chars`

## Corporate / explainer video

**Constraints:** 1–3 minutes · under voiceover · neutral, non-distracting

```
Corporate ambient, calm and credible, no vocals, 104 BPM, soft marimba, warm pads, light pulse, even dynamics, no peaks
```

`119 chars`

`even dynamics, no peaks` is what keeps the music from fighting the narration. This is the one context where "unremarkable" is the actual goal.

## Wedding

**Constraints:** 2–4 minutes · lyrics matter here · usually one specific moment (aisle, first dance)

**Processional (instrumental):**

```
Classical crossover, tender, no vocals, 72 BPM, solo piano and strings, simple memorable melody, builds gently, warm
```

`116 chars`

**First dance (with vocals):**

```
Acoustic soul, romantic, male and female duet, 76 BPM, nylon guitar, close harmony, brushed drums, intimate mix
```

`111 chars`

> **Description:** A song about ordinary shared mornings rather than grand declarations — the specific details of a life two people already share.

Specificity in the description is what separates a personal wedding song from a generic love song. Name real details.

## Study / focus

**Constraints:** 20+ minutes ideally · no vocals · no attention-grabbing events

```
Lo-fi hip hop, calm, no vocals, 76 BPM, dusty piano loop, soft drums, vinyl crackle, repetitive, no melody changes
```

`114 chars`

`no melody changes` is doing real work. Focus music fails when something novel happens — novelty is exactly what pulls attention away from the task.

**Non-lo-fi variant:**

```
Minimal ambient, neutral, no vocals, 60 BPM, sustained pads, gentle motion, no percussion, no melodic hooks, seamless
```

`117 chars`

## Sleep / relaxation

**Constraints:** long · no vocals · no dynamic range · nothing above about 4 kHz

```
Ambient sleep, peaceful, no vocals, 50 BPM, deep warm pads, very slow drift, low-passed, no percussion, no transients
```

`117 chars`

`no transients` matters more than anything else here. Any sharp attack — a piano note, a rim shot — can pull a listener out of a light sleep stage.

---

## Meditation / yoga

**Constraints:** 10–30 minutes · no vocals or wordless only · steady, non-directional

```
Meditative ambient, serene, wordless vocal pad, 56 BPM, singing bowls, drone, soft flute, no rhythm, timeless feel
```

`114 chars`

`no rhythm` removes the sense of elapsed time, which is the point. `timeless feel` reinforces it.

## Workout

**Constraints:** matched to activity BPM · vocals fine and often helpful · consistent energy

| Activity | Target BPM |
|----------|-----------|
| Warm-up / cool-down | 100–115 |
| Steady cardio | 125–140 |
| HIIT intervals | 145–165 |
| Heavy lifting | 90–110 (heavy feel, not fast) |

```
Gym trap, aggressive, male vocal with ad-libs, 150 BPM, hard 808, sharp hats, relentless energy, no slow sections
```

`113 chars`

`no slow sections` prevents the mid-track breakdown that ruins an interval.

**Lifting variant** — heavy rather than fast:

```
Heavy hip-hop, menacing, no vocals, 96 BPM, distorted 808, half-time feel, dark brass, huge low end, relentless
```

`111 chars`

## Retail / café ambience

**Constraints:** hours of playback · no vocals in most contexts · pleasant but forgettable

```
Bossa nova lounge, pleasant, no vocals, 118 BPM, nylon guitar, light shaker, soft Rhodes, warm, unobtrusive, no solos
```

`117 chars`

`no solos` prevents the moments that make a listener look up. In commercial spaces, that is a defect and not a feature.

## Personalised gift song

**Constraints:** lyrics carry all the value · 2–3 minutes · vocals essential

```
Acoustic pop, warm and sincere, male vocal, 92 BPM, fingerpicked guitar, light strings, intimate close-mic, no drums
```

`116 chars`

> **Description:** Write about a specific person using concrete details only — the mug they always use, the road they always take, the phrase they repeat. No general praise, no abstractions.

This is the one use case where the **description field matters more than the style field**. Generic praise produces a generic song. Three specific details produce something the recipient recognises as theirs.
