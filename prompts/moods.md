# Mood Prompts

Mood is the slot people over-invest in and under-specify. `sad` is not an instruction — a sad piano nocturne and a sad country ballad share almost no production decisions.

So this page is organized differently from [genres.md](genres.md): each mood gets **two contrasting arrangements**, to show that the emotional target is reached through instrumentation and tempo, not through adjectives. Pick whichever route fits your project, or read both to see which knobs actually moved.

## Cheerful

**Route A — bright and busy**

```
Indie pop, cheerful, mixed vocals, 126 BPM, ukulele, handclaps, glockenspiel, whistling hook, sunny major key
```

`109 chars` — Cheerfulness through *density*: lots of small bright events. `whistling hook` is the single most effective cheerful-signalling tag there is.

**Route B — relaxed and warm**

```
Soul pop, cheerful, female vocal, 98 BPM, warm horns, easy shuffle groove, electric piano, no urgency
```

`101 chars` — Same mood at two-thirds the tempo. Cheerful does not have to mean fast; `no urgency` keeps the groove from tightening up.

## Melancholic

**Route A — sparse and close**

```
Piano ballad, melancholic, female vocal, 68 BPM, felt piano, close-mic breathy delivery, room reverb, no drums
```

`110 chars` — `no drums` is the whole trick. Removing the rhythmic floor is what makes sadness feel unsupported.

**Route B — full band, held back**

```
Alternative rock, melancholic, male vocal, 88 BPM, clean guitar, restrained drums, cello underneath, never fully opens
```

`118 chars` — `never fully opens` denies the release the arrangement keeps promising. Withheld resolution reads as melancholy far more than minor chords do.

## Healing

**Route A — ambient**

```
Ambient, healing, no vocals, 60 BPM, sustained warm pads, soft piano, distant strings, gentle swells, no percussion
```

`115 chars` — `no percussion` removes any sense of being moved along. Healing music should not have a deadline.

**Route B — acoustic**

```
Acoustic folk, healing, soft female vocal, 76 BPM, nylon guitar, light strings, humming, warm intimate mix
```

`106 chars` — `humming` adds a human presence without adding lyrical content. Useful when words would be too specific.

## Energetic

**Route A — electronic**

```
Future bass, energetic, no vocals, 150 BPM, punchy supersaw chords, sidechained pumping, snappy snare, rising builds
```

`116 chars` — `pumping` names the sidechain effect that *is* the genre's sense of forward motion.

**Route B — live band**

```
Funk rock, energetic, male vocal, 118 BPM, slap bass, tight horn stabs, wah guitar, live drums, no quantisation
```

`111 chars` — Energy from *groove* rather than tempo. `no quantisation` keeps the push-and-pull that makes live funk feel alive.

## Romantic

**Route A — classic**

```
Soul ballad, romantic, male vocal, 64 BPM, lush strings, warm Rhodes, brushed drums, rich harmonies, vinyl warmth
```

`113 chars` — `lush` plus `rich harmonies` is the classic-soul signature. Both terms are reliably interpreted.

**Route B — modern and understated**

```
Indie folk, romantic, duet vocals, 84 BPM, two acoustic guitars, close harmony, minimal production, room noise
```

`110 chars` — `room noise` sounds like a flaw and functions as intimacy. Small imperfections read as authenticity.

## Dark

**Route A — cinematic**

```
Dark cinematic, ominous, no vocals, 70 BPM, low drone, detuned strings, sub hits, sparse percussion, dissonant swells
```

`117 chars` — `drone` establishes dread through sustained tension rather than through events.

**Route B — electronic**

```
Industrial techno, dark, no vocals, 132 BPM, distorted kick, metallic percussion, cold atmosphere, relentless loop
```

`114 chars` — `relentless` prevents the arrangement from introducing relief. Dark tracks break when the model adds a hopeful bridge.

## Dreamy

**Route A — shoegaze**

```
Dreamgaze, dreamy, distant female vocal, 92 BPM, walls of reverbed guitar, vocals buried in mix, hazy, no clarity
```

`113 chars` — `buried in mix` is a mix instruction, not a tone one. Placement creates the dream state.

**Route B — synth**

```
Synth pop, dreamy, soft vocals, 104 BPM, warm analog pads, chorus effect, slow arpeggios, wide stereo field, no harsh transients
```

`128 chars` — `no harsh transients` removes every sharp edge. Dreaminess is largely the absence of attack.

## Lonely

**Route A — one instrument**

```
Solo piano, lonely, no vocals, 62 BPM, single piano, long pauses, sustain pedal, empty hall reverb
```

`98 chars` — `long pauses` is the instruction. Silence is the loneliest thing a mix can contain, and models will not leave it unless asked.

**Route B — voice in a big space**

```
Ambient folk, lonely, male vocal, 74 BPM, one acoustic guitar, vast reverb, no accompaniment, distant field recording
```

`117 chars` — The contrast between a small source and a large space is what loneliness sounds like.

## Gentle

**Route A — lullaby**

```
Lullaby, gentle, soft female vocal, 66 BPM, music box, light strings, humming, very quiet dynamics, no percussion
```

`113 chars` — `very quiet dynamics` asks for a narrow volume range. Gentleness is a dynamics property before it is a timbre one.

**Route B — acoustic**

```
Acoustic, gentle, whispered vocal, 80 BPM, fingerpicked nylon guitar, soft brushes, warm low-passed mix, no sharp attacks
```

`121 chars` — `soft brushes` keeps a pulse without introducing impact.

## Ethereal

**Route A — choral**

```
Ethereal choral, otherworldly, wordless choir, 56 BPM, layered vowel pads, cathedral reverb, slow drift, no rhythm
```

`114 chars` — `wordless` frees the voice from language, and `no rhythm` frees it from time. Both are needed.

**Route B — electronic**

```
Ambient electronic, ethereal, processed vocal textures, 68 BPM, granular pads, shimmer reverb, high airy frequencies, weightless
```

`128 chars` — `shimmer reverb` is a specific, widely-recognised effect; `weightless` sets the overall target.

## Nostalgic

**Route A — tape era**

```
Retro pop, nostalgic, male vocal, 112 BPM, analog synths, tape saturation, gated snare, slight pitch wobble, 1980s
```

`114 chars` — `slight pitch wobble` is what old tape actually did. Simulating the medium's flaws is the most direct route to nostalgia.

**Route B — sampled memory**

```
Lo-fi soul, nostalgic, distant vocal sample, 80 BPM, chopped vintage sample, vinyl crackle, muffled highs, warm dust
```

`116 chars` — `muffled highs` mimics how memory degrades, which is why lo-fi reads as remembering rather than hearing.

## Intense

**Route A — orchestral**

```
Epic orchestral, intense, no vocals, 140 BPM, aggressive strings, war drums, brass stabs, relentless build, no release
```

`118 chars` — `no release` sustains pressure across the whole track instead of spending it at the first peak.

**Route B — heavy band**

```
Post-hardcore, intense, screamed vocal, 164 BPM, dense distorted guitars, double kick, wall of sound, maximum energy
```

`116 chars` — `wall of sound` sets the density target; the tempo and double kick supply the rate.

## Combining moods

Two moods can be stacked when they are in *tension* rather than agreement — that tension is often what makes a track memorable.

| Pairing | Why it works |
|---------|--------------|
| `nostalgic, hopeful` | Memory with a forward pull instead of pure regret |
| `dark, dreamy` | Menace without aggression — good for tension scenes |
| `energetic, melancholic` | The classic "sad banger"; fast tempo, minor key, defeated lyrics |
| `gentle, ominous` | Quiet dread. Effective in games and thriller trailers |

Stacking two moods that agree (`sad, melancholic, lonely`) wastes characters — they collapse into one signal. Spend those characters on production detail instead.
